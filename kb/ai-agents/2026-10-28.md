## arXiv: EvoRouter: Cost-Efficient Multi-Agent Routing via Insight-Guided On-Policy Distillation
- We propose EvoRouter, a self-evolving multi-agent routing framework driven by insight-guided on-policy distillation.
- Serving web queries at scale with multi-agent systems requires jointly configuring large language models, collaboration modes, agent counts, interaction rounds, and skills.
- However, the resulting configuration space grows combinatorially, while sparse execution rewards provide limited fine-grained supervision for interdependent planning decisions.
- A dedicated expert periodically synthesizes performance-cost feedback from configuration trajectories across tasks into reusable conditional routing insights about capability requirements, returns from resource scaling, and interactions among configuration choices.
- A privileged teacher combines these evolving insights with current execution feedback to provide dense token-level supervision for the router's own planning trajectories through on-policy distillation.

## arXiv: Simulation-Efficient Double-Flow Neural Posterior Estimation for Agent-Based Models
- We propose Double-Flow Neural Posterior Estimation (DF-NPE), which combines learned time-series summaries with two conditional normalizing flows for synthetic-data generation and posterior estimation.
- Neural posterior estimation (NPE) enables Bayesian inference of the posterior parameter in agent-based models (ABMs) with intractable likelihoods, but simulating training data can be computationally expensive.
- The framework supplements direct simulations with synthetic training pairs and supports both pooled and ordered training strategies.
- Experiments in two settings of the Brock-Hommes asset-pricing model show that DF-NPE can reduce direct simulations by 90 percent while achieving lower mean posterior discrepancies and runtime than standard NPE.

## arXiv: Knowing When to Escalate: Disagreement Patterns for Uncertainty-Aware Content Moderation
- We propose Reasoning Trace Divergence Analysis (RTDA), which embeds model-generated justifications from perspective-differentiated agents and classifies each case by justification similarity and conclusion agreement.
- Agentic content-moderation systems increasingly rely on collective judgments from LLM agents, while aggregation methods commonly treat inter-agent disagreement as a reliability problem to be removed through consensus or voting.
- This framing is inappropriate for value-laden tasks, where disagreement may reflect irreducible value pluralism - a distinction standard uncertainty decompositions cannot recover, since they operate on output distributions and discard the reasoning behind each judgment.
- The resulting four-state taxonomy isolates convergent disagreement, where agents reasoning similarly but concluding differently, as a candidate signature of value-laden conflict.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.

## arXiv: Guarded Agent-in-the-Loop Residual Adaptation for Evolving Wind Power Regimes
- We present a guarded Agent-in-the-loop residual adaptation framework that leaves the temporal backbone unchanged and adapts only a small set of scene-conditioned residual experts.
- Wind power forecasting models are often deployed as fixed temporal backbones, but the errors they make can vary across meteorological regimes such as cold waves, heat-and-calm periods, productive typhoon, and destructive storms.
- An Agent layer interprets external scene information, identifies the current regime, selects residual experts, and applies a correction only when recent observed errors support it.
- New experts enter the pool after they outperform their initializations on a validation set, keeping model growth under explicit control.

## arXiv: Collective Emergent Alignment: Measuring and Amplifying Interaction-Induced Reasoning Alignment in Multi-Agent LLMs

## arXiv: DynaFlow: Learning Free-Form Multi-Agent Orchestration
- We introduce DynaFlow, a framework that integrates free-form executable workflow synthesis into the action space of the main agent.
- Multi-agent systems often rely on predefined collaboration structures, limiting their ability to adapt to diverse task requirements.
