## arXiv: GBClaw: An agentic AI framework for atomistic grain boundary research and mechanistic discovery
- Here, we present GBClaw, an agentic AI framework for investigating the structure and deformation of general grain boundaries (GBs).
- Agentic artificial intelligence (AI) systems hold immense potential for automating complex workflows in materials discovery and design, but extending them to domain-intensive mechanistic investigations remains challenging.

## arXiv: Proof-Carrying Contracts: Commitments AI Agents Cannot Break
- We introduce Proof-Carrying Contracts (PC2), a framework for making agreed constraints enforceable throughout an agent's interactions with a system.
- AI agents increasingly act on behalf of people or other agents, purchasing services, exchanging information and delegating work.
- What an agent must respect is written in instructions, policies or agreements, but text alone cannot make it comply.
- It may make mistakes, be redirected by what it reads, or pursue different interests.
- For an economy of such agents to function, parties need agreements they can rely on without inspecting or trusting one another's agents.

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.

## arXiv: Claims of emergence in AI should be grounded in information decomposition
- When a language model develops capabilities not traceable to its individual components, when a multi-agent AI system outperforms each agent on its own, or when a human-AI pair succeeds where either fails in isolation, AI researchers describe it as a case of ``synergy'' or ``emergence''.
- However, these are usually informal assessments not supported by a theoretical framework, making these claims impossible to compare or even meaningfully dispute.
- Here we argue that \emph{information decomposition can serve as a unifying formal framework to operationalize assessments about synergy in AI systems}.

**Why it matters:** Tightens the multi-agent systems literature on a specific design axis.

## arXiv: Local Work, Global Progress: A Convergence Theory of AI Agent Collaboration
- Teams of large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly applied to complex tasks, but their collaboration protocols are mostly designed empirically, without convergence guarantees.
- We formalize collaborative problem solving as block optimization: each agent improves one part of a shared solution, guided by its local objective plus a correction for how its changes affect the other parts.
- A coordinator checks every proposal against the global objective and merges proposals that do not interfere with each other.
- We prove linear convergence up to an error floor that vanishes when the corrections are exact.

**Why it matters:** Useful baseline / analysis for single-agent capability boundaries.

**Tags:** #single-agent #planning #protocol #evaluation
