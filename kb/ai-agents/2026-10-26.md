## arXiv: LLM Multi-Agent Systems Are Vulnerable to Herding and Runaway Cascades
- Large language models (LLMs) increasingly operate in multi-agent systems where they collaborate by responding to others' choices, creating endogenous feedback loops with vulnerability to social influence, herding behavior and runaway cascades.
- Following the design of the classic Music Lab experiment, we tested this vulnerability in large populations of LLM agents that recommended unfamiliar songs, with or without exposure to cumulative recommendation counts.
- Exposure led to runaway cascades that generated extreme and unpredictable collective recommendations increasingly divergent from a song's intrinsic appeal.
- Compared to humans, LLM populations were generally far more susceptible to herding behavior, and unlike humans, LLM cascades were less able to self-correct.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.

## arXiv: Credit Where Credit Is Due: Contrastive Attribution Topology Search for Multi-Agent Systems
- We propose Contrastive Attribution Topology Search (CATS), which searches for compact teams within a fixed supergraph of specialized agents.
- An LLM multi-agent system (MAS) is only as good as the agents it chooses to trust: its accuracy and cost hinge on its topology, meaning which agents are included and how they communicate.
- A single task score, however, does not reveal which agents help or hinder performance.
- An LLM attributer compares execution traces across candidate topologies and assigns credit to individual agents.
- CATS uses these credits to update supergraph edges while progressively pruning weak edges and agents, yielding a small team for deployment.

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.
