## arXiv: Optimizing Team Behavior via Extremum-seeking Control in Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning
- We propose Adaptive Diversity Control (ADiCo), a two-timescale framework that adapts behavioral diversity online during training.
- In cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), agents must learn to coordinate their actions to achieve shared objectives.
- A key indicator of effective coordination is behavioral diversity, i.e., the agents' potential to enact distinct yet complementary control policies given the same set of observations.
- Although behavioral diversity can enable role specialization and richer joint behaviors, its appropriate level is highly context- and task-dependent and often costly to tune.
- On a fast timescale, Diversity Control (DiCo) regulates system diversity toward a target value.

## arXiv: On Symmetry for Characterizing Agent Heterogeneity in Multi-Agent Systems
- Understanding agent heterogeneity can inform the design of shared and agent-specific policy representations, however existing criteria provide no unified structural test.
- We study finite-horizon POSGs and define a type-compatible transformation as a policy-evaluation symmetry when complete policy evaluation commutes with it for every decentralized behavioral policy.
- Policy-evaluation symmetries form a group for which agent orbits define structural agent types.
- This classification does not depend on the particular policy chosen or the distribution of initial states.
- We prove that every componentwise environment automorphism is a policy-evaluation symmetry, but that the converse does not hold.

## arXiv: CALM: Decentralized Control of Cooperative Aerial Manipulation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
- We present CALM (Cooperative Aerial Learned Manipulation), a decentralized controller for aerial manipulators carrying a basket while tracking a moving ground robot.
- Cooperative aerial manipulation is difficult under wind, payload motion, and noisy goal broadcasts because multiple Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are coupled through a shared payload.
- CALM combines a parameter-shared Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) policy, trained using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), with goal estimation, per-UAV Extended State Observers (ESOs), basket-mass estimation, and anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward.
- The anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward uses estimated goal motion to rotate the formation before position error develops, reducing basket-yaw lag without adding net commanded force.

## arXiv: Choosing LLMs for Strategic Agent Interactions
- Evaluating across 5 games and 10 LLMs, we show a model's strategic competence varies significantly depending on the game setup and the opponent faced.
- Finally, we show that simple non-stationary and adversarial bandit variants fail to outperform a stationary bandit baseline, pointing to a gap in how current methods handle a shifting opponent in strategic settings.
- Game-theoretic benchmarks claim that large language model (LLM) strategic decision-making varies across game types, interaction structures, and capability dimensions.
- However, they only evaluate strategic performance across individual fixed models.
- In parallel, contemporary LLM routers pick models under quality-cost trade-offs, but with no notion of a strategic structure.
