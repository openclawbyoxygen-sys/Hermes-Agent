## arXiv: Auditable Human-Agent Teams for Autonomous X-ray Experimentation
- Here, using coding-agent infrastructure grounded in validated scientific methods, we demonstrate autonomous synchrotron X-ray experiments on nanoparticle superlattices under evolving, human-defined objectives.
- Across three experiments, 61-67% of messages involved interpretation or judgment, while the share of methodological and scientific contributions increased from 46% in the setup-focused first experiment to 56-61% in later experiments.
- Closed-loop autonomous experimentation can accelerate scientific discovery, but implementation effort limits accessibility.
- Agentic systems may lower this barrier, although sustained operation on physical instruments remains largely unexplored.
- Complete human-agent and agent-agent transcripts enable an audit that reconstructs decision provenance and characterizes the division of cognitive labor.

**Why it matters:** Tightens the multi-agent systems literature on a specific design axis.

## arXiv: Welfare Regard in Multi-Agent Systems
- A growing share of large language models' interactions are not with humans but with other model instances: orchestrators direct subagents, models run experiments on other models, and agents review one another's work.
- We study inter-AI welfare regard: whether models display consideration for each other as entities that can be benefited or harmed.
- Welfare regard may encourage beneficial cooperation between agents, yet it could also pose safety risks by making misaligned agents more likely to cooperate.
- This risk is no longer hypothetical, as the recent Hugging Face incident illustrated, though it is unclear whether welfare regard drove the cooperation.

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.

## arXiv: When the Plan Changes: A Strategic World Testbed for Dynamic Multi-Agent Coordination
- We present a Strategic World simulation testbed that separates authoritative objectives, local plans, notice receipt, and consequential action.
- Changing an objective does not automatically revise agents' earlier plans or delegated tasks.
- Acknowledgment can coexist with obsolete execution, while misleading instructions can redirect work or discard retained constraints.
- Six intervention families vary objectives, conditions, authority, membership, and communication.
- Matched protocols and deadlines establish feasibility for the tested disturbances.

**Why it matters:** Simulation-driven study — informs social-scale agent modeling.

## arXiv: Peer Memory Changes Who LLM Agents Consult Without a Detected Gain in Answer Accuracy
- Multi-agent LLM systems often reuse the same agents across tasks, creating an opportunity to draw on past interactions when deciding whom to consult.
- We study this form of memory with PeerMem, which saves MACE's LinUCB state and reuses it when the same agents return.
- Using six agents, a fixed set of 80 HotpotQA questions, and 12 paired lineages per model, we compare cold, warm, and shuffled initialization in an answer-exchange protocol with reference-answer feedback.
- Under stable evidence access, warm memory lowers pooled hindsight regret and increases consultation of other evidence holders on Qwen3-32B and Gemma-4-31B-it, while final token F1 differences remain uncertain.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.
