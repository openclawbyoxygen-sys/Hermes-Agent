## arXiv: Rare, Relevant, and Still Wrong: How Memory Fails LLM Group Decisions
- We introduce CrossProfile, a diagnostic benchmark of 60 templates with automatically verified answer keys, in which the ownership of supporting, decoy, and irrelevant facts is crossed while their text is held fixed (480 tasks).
- When an LLM group's discussion is compressed into a short memory for a final decision maker, what should the memory keep?
- Two natural heuristics keep the rarest claims or the claims relevant to the decision criteria.
- On hidden-profile tasks both look attractive, because how widely a fact is held is confounded with whether it helps.
- Across Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and two DeepSeek models, both heuristics fail in predictable places.

**Why it matters:** RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) contribution.

## arXiv: TANDEM: Balancing Centralized and Decentralized Critics to Address the Information Gap in Complex Multi-Agent Environments
- We propose TANDEM, which keeps one centralized critic and reads it two ways at each training step: a centralized read from the global state, and a decentralized read restricted to the information each agent will have at execution.
- Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning under centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) shapes decentralized policies with a centralized critic that observes the global state, then removes the critic at execution.
- The policies are optimized to rely on information they will not have when they act, an information gap between training and execution.
- It widens under partial observability, observation noise, and openness, where the set of agents and tasks present changes during an episode.

## arXiv: Compatibility and future-proof testing for multi-agent systems
- Independently developed autonomous agents, such as autonomous vehicles, must guarantee joint compatibility, such as safety and task-completion requirements, when they operate in a shared environment.
- Existing methods require a centrally designed specification, or interaction mechanism before any agent is deployed.
- However, such centralized design is unrealistic when agent designs are confidential to their developers, and it is not flexible enough to accommodate future agent designs.
- We study a bi-level structure in which a regulator coordinates developers by publishing seed agents, and each developer independently tests its agent to ensure that it is compatible with every seed-compatible agent, a property we call future-proofness.

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.
