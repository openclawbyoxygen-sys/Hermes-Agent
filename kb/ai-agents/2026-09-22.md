## arXiv: Bayesian Belief Layer for Controllable Opinion Dynamics in LLM Agents
- We introduce Bayesian Chronicle Agents (BCA), a minimal belief layer separating \emph{what} an agent believes from \emph{how} it speaks.
- LLM agents in social simulation revise their opinions implicitly, in context: how open an agent is to persuasion can neither be specified nor verified, and collective outcomes inherit the model's training prior.
- Each stance is a probability, updated by one Bayesian step per utterance heard.
- A single prior-strength parameter $κ$ encodes stubbornness, modeled after its role in Friedkin--Johnsen (FJ) opinion dynamics.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.

## arXiv: Loopjacking: Hijacking Human-in-the-Loop Approval
- We separate this contribution from established work on misleading dialogs, session smuggling, action binding, and authorization continuity.
- Human approval is often treated as the last security boundary before an agent executes a consequential operation.
- That boundary is only meaningful if the operation presented for review is the operation later authorized or released.
- We call failures of this binding Loopjacking: a human approves what they understand as operation A, while the implementation uses that decision for a materially different operation B.

**Why it matters:** Orchestration / coordination of many agents.
