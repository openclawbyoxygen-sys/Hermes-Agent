## arXiv: When Is a Memory Safe Enough to Act On? Action-Conditioned Verification for Long-Lived Agents
- Long-lived agents act on memory carried across sessions, and many of their actions are irreversible, so a stale memory can turn directly into harm.
- Existing work improves the memory itself --- retrieving, refreshing, or reconciling entries --- yet whether an entry must be re-checked before acting is not a property of the entry: the same remembered address can be used unchecked for a routine shipment but should be re-confirmed before a valuable one.
- We therefore study \emph{action-conditioned memory verification}: given a candidate action, decide which of its memory premises to re-verify and when to proceed or abstain, balancing execution risk, abstention, and verification cost.

**Why it matters:** Tool-use pattern — relevant to agent-tool integration research.

## arXiv: Conservative Meta-Agent Search: Architecture Adaptation under Non-Stationarity
- We formalize meta-agent architecture search as sequential incumbent replacement with switching costs and hard risk constraints and introduce Conservative Meta-Agent Search (CMAS).
- Since backtests cannot certify meta-level skill, we introduce MetaInvest-Bench, a regime-switching simulator whose specified worker competence makes a surrogate-optimal architecture computable, with leakage-controlled replay, adversarial governance tracks, and five negative controls.
- CMAS retrieves archived architectures and proposes typed graph edits, then scores each against the incumbent by paired shadow replay under common random numbers, with interventional component credit.
- Automated agentic-system design is typically evaluated on static task suites with immediate scalar rewards.
- Deployed systems face persistent shift, delayed dependent feedback, correlated component failures, and costly architecture changes.

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.

## arXiv: Probabilistic Energy Modeling Unifies Versatile Swarm Control
- Here we show that probabilistic energy modeling provides a unifying representation for versatile swarm control, in which diverse missions emerge from compositions of shared interaction principles rather than task-specific control formulations.
- Robotic swarms must coordinate across diverse missions, yet existing controllers often require redesign, retuning, or retraining when objectives change.
- By exploiting the correspondence between Gibbs random field energy minimization and structured reward maximization, we encode fundamental swarm behaviors as reusable energy terms within a unified probabilistic objective.
- Decentralized policies trained within this representation are composed into a single controller through lightweight policy gating, without retraining.

**Why it matters:** Simulation-driven study — informs social-scale agent modeling.
