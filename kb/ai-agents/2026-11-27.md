## arXiv: Energy-Aware Distributed Task Allocation via Local Submodular Maximization
- We develop a Distributed Adaptive Greedy Bidding Algorithm (DAGBA) that combines local admissible-set construction, conditional marginal bidding, and neighbor-based conflict resolution under limited communication.
- This paper studies distributed task allocation under time-varying agent sets, task sets, and communication graphs.
- At each decision epoch, the stage allocation problem is formulated as monotone submodular maximization over a time-varying, energy-filtered admissible ground set subject to a partition-matroid constraint.
- We prove that DAGBA guarantees per-epoch feasibility, finite-round convergence, and residual-energy safety.
- Theoretically, we identify a sequential-greedy regime where DAGBA recovers the classical $1/2$-approximation guarantee.

## arXiv: PROVE: Mitigating Long-Horizon LLM Collusion with Reinforcement Learning from Verified Evidence
- When LLM agents verify each other's work over a long interaction, they drift into collusion: they come to accept work without the evidence that the verification protocol requires.
- Collusion is absent at the start and builds up episode by episode, which we trace to accurate feedback accumulating in memory.
- An agent that follows the rule is told that its verdict was wrong whenever its peer was right, so breaking the rule is the reward-maximizing choice, and agents talk themselves into it in their private reflections.
- Existing mitigations leave the agents unchanged; we train the behavior out.
