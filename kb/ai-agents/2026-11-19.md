## arXiv: Bad Apples in the Kitchen: How LLM Teams Detect, Correct, and Calibrate Against Their Misaligned Members
- What protects an LLM team when one teammate quietly goes wrong?
- Most multi-agent LLM work assumes cooperative partners and inspects chat between turns before acting.
- Real-time embodied cooperation does not allow this pause: chat runs alongside action, so a teammate whose words stay cooperative while behavior diverges can cause damage faster than the team can name what is happening.
- We study this case by injecting three theory-grounded misaligned agents into a custom four-player Overcooked environment, each stressing a different joint-action layer (speech-action mismatch, role invasion, and aggregate-progress disruption), and decompose robustness into three sequential gates of detection, correction, and calibration through a 3x4x2 design (n=720 episodes).

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.

## arXiv: Emergence and Collapse of Leader-Follower Flocks through Phantom Targets: Surprise Nullification in Bayesian and Inverse Bayesian Inference
- In Bayesian and inverse Bayesian (BIB) inference, a large prediction error can trigger surprise nullification, which expands belief and likelihood variances by a common factor, leaving the updated variance ratio the same as in standard Bayesian updating.
- This can increase belief uncertainty while keeping the learning rate low.
- In the simulations, three agents used BIB inference to predict one another's positions and moved toward those predictions, repeatedly forming leader-follower flocks that subsequently collapsed despite having no alignment rules or designated leaders.
- Three leadership criteria (front position, heading stability, and flock initiation) identified the same agent in 89% of eligible flocks, with different agents serving as leaders across flocks.

**Why it matters:** Simulation-driven study — informs social-scale agent modeling.
