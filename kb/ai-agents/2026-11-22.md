## arXiv: Staged Semantic Distillation: Preserving Free-Form Semantics in a Low-Compute Non-LLM Surrogate of a Staged LLM Agent
- We present staged semantic distillation, a low-compute method that preserves non-structured semantics in a non-LLM student trained from one recorded trace.
- The paper is a method-and-case-study contribution on one simulator and trace.
- Staged LLM agents make market and social simulators realistic, but one 102-day run of a 200-agent, ten-index market simulator issues 203,100 language-model calls and returns one realization.
- Conventional low-compute distillation drops their free-form intermediate state.
- A frozen encoder maps each teacher output to a 1024-d vector; the student sees typed stage-tagged vectors with stage-specific visibility, is jointly supervised, and trains with scheduled self-history.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.

## arXiv: When Reviews Gate Access: Collusion, Sabotage and the Scale of Peer Review in the AI Submission Surge
- AI tools have helped push submissions to the largest machine-learning venues to tens of thousands per cycle.
- Venues have answered with rules that tie access to review: per-author caps, mandatory reciprocal reviewing, reviewer eligibility earned through accepted papers, and assignment based on reviewer bids.
- Under these rules a reviewer is also a competitor of the authors.
- We study two deviations that the rules make attractive.
- We build an agent-based conference calibrated to public statistics of NeurIPS and ICLR and run 6,220 simulated conferences.

**Why it matters:** Tool-use pattern — relevant to agent-tool integration research.

**Tags:** #tool-use #simulation #safety

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19054
