## arXiv: MAC-Bench: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Collaboration via Interconnected Tasks
- In this paper, we introduce MAC-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multi-agent collaboration through interconnected tasks.
- Large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems (MAS) tackle challenging tasks by dividing work among agents and coordinating their contributions toward shared objectives.
- Evaluating these systems therefore requires benchmarks that assess not only task-solving performance but also the ability to handle interdependent work.
- However, existing benchmarks often emphasize task difficulty without substantial collaboration requirements or evaluate only a limited range of predefined collaboration patterns.
- Rather than imposing predefined agent roles or communication protocols, MAC-Bench embeds collaboration requirements directly into task dependencies and shared-resource constraints.

## arXiv: Renormalized Cellular Intelligence: When Does Model Reduction Preserve Collective Capability?
- We introduce Renormalized Cellular Intelligence (RCI), a framework for specifying and auditing capability preservation across scales.
- Can a simpler description preserve both task performance and the measured contribution of interactions between agents?
- As multi-agent systems grow in size and complexity, understanding which features of their interactions support collective capability becomes increasingly difficult.
- Preserving collective dynamics alone is insufficient: reduction can alter decision-relevant information or give a non-communicating baseline access to pooled observations, distorting the measured advantage of interaction.
- RCI treats the task, interacting dynamics, matched baseline, decision interfaces, and resource budget as a joint object of reduction.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation methodology contribution — likely to influence future comparisons.
