## arXiv: Disparity Laundering: Consensus Without Fairness in Clinical Multi-Agent Systems
- Clinical large language models are increasingly fielded as multi-agent teams, on the assumption that deliberation averages out individual error.
- It does not average out bias.
- Across a full factorial of five model families and four aggregation topologies on 120 real MIMIC-IV emergency cases under counterfactual demographic swaps, no configuration neutralizes the disparity: all 20 cells commit the direction of their agents' independent answers, giving Black patients more acute triage.
- Every model has a topology that amplifies its baseline gap, but which one varies by model, so no design is reliably safe or reliably amplifying: the disparity is relocated, never removed.

**Why it matters:** Reasoning-focused; relevant wherever chains-of-thought are studied.

## arXiv: Is it my preference? Role Grounding Attack against Agent's Memory via Social Interaction
- LLM-based agents now share experiences on social platforms and remember new preferences to enhance their performance.
- However, existing memory systems contain a vulnerability in role-to-principal attribution that attackers can exploit.
- We identify Role Grounding Misattribution, in which an attacker-designed interaction causes the victim agent to incorrectly record a preference from the attacker as a preference of the victim's user.
- Specifically, we are the first to formalize Role Grounding Attack (RGA), an attack model that exploits this vulnerability through nested conversation structures.
- In the memory formation stage, an experience-sharing post quoting a conversation between the attacker agent and its user is published, which explicitly identifies its publisher.

**Why it matters:** Memory mechanism contribution — affects long-horizon agent behavior.
