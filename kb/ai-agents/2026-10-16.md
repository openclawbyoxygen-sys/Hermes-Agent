## arXiv: The Wealth of Agents: Scaling Agentic AI Across Developers
- Today's agentic systems are typically designed, built, and deployed by single developers with full control over all participating agents.
- While this simplifies development, it limits the potential scale of agentic systems.
- We study multi-developer agentic systems (MDAs) which enable developers to collaboratively build agentic systems to push frontier capability beyond what any individual developer could achieve alone.
- We construct empirical and theoretical models of developers to compare MDAs with single-developer agentic systems (SDAs).
- On GAIA, a general assistant benchmark, a population of specialized developers whose individual agents do not exceed 35% accuracy reaches 70% when their agents are combined in an MDA.

**Why it matters:** RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) contribution.

## arXiv: What and Where in Identifying and Quantifying Conventions in Humans and AI Agents
- Cooperative success rests in part on implicit communication.
- Partners converge on conventions: regularities that give an action meaning beyond its direct effect.
- Yet conventions are usually observed only indirectly: a score drop when partners are swapped shows that a convention existed, but not what it was or where it was used.
- In the card game Hanabi, the exact probability that a play fails can be computed from the hints received and the cards in view.
- Players who use conventions succeed more often than this prediction allows; the resulting margin is the convention gap.

**Why it matters:** Tagged #agents — relevant to that sub-area.

**Tags:** #agents

## arXiv: Drivers of LLM Consensus Formation Under Competing Incentives: A Simulation Study
- We introduce an evaluation, based on the Network Consensus game, to assess the mechanisms driving this process.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent environments that produce unanimous agreement, both in intended interactions that need coordination and in unintended emergent interactions.
