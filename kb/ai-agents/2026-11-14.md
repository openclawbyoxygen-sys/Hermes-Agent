## arXiv: Evidence-Governed Adoption of Self-Generated Updates in Autonomous AI Agents
- We introduce Evidence-Governed Evolution (EGE), a framework for governing adoption of self-generated agent modifications.
- Autonomous agents modify prompts, memories, tools, workflows, and policies using interaction histories.
- Yet a plausible modification is not necessarily suitable for persistent adoption: candidate selection is adaptive, evaluators may be imperfect, and local gains can hide degradation against an earlier baseline.
- EGE separates candidate generation from confirmatory adoption, evaluates frozen candidates on fresh paired audits, requires meaningful improvement on a prespecified primary objective, protects declared capabilities through reference-relative non-inferiority tests, and applies governance constraints before bounded canary deployment.
- We establish an evaluator-calibrated simultaneous lower-bound construction for a countable sequence of adaptively generated candidates.

**Why it matters:** Planning/decision-making contribution under agent settings.

## arXiv: Shared Item-Level Error Limits the Aggregation of Small Language Models in Fermi Estimation
- Multi-agent systems of language models are often motivated by an averaging argument: if the agents' errors are unbiased and independent, the error of an aggregate of N answers falls as 1/sqrt(N).
- Fermi estimation, whose continuous answers are aggregated on the logarithmic scale, permits a direct test.

## arXiv: SwarmSafe-Delta: Isolating Message Effects on LLM Agent Safety
- We introduce SwarmSafe-Delta, a paired executable protocol that freezes upstream computation, varies real, hidden, and format-matched placebo delivery, and scores a single committed action against deterministic benign and risky policy states under preassigned budgets.
- The contribution is a controlled, execution-grounded measurement protocol rather than a general claim that agent communication is harmful or protective.
- When a tool-using LLM agent team violates policy, aggregate outcomes do not reveal whether an upstream message changed the executor's action.
- Across four models and constructed optional tasks, real delivery at $N=4$ produces 21.4% risky-world violations, versus 5.0% with placebo and 1.0% with hidden delivery (paired differences +16.4 and +20.3 percentage points).

**Why it matters:** Safety / alignment implication for deployed agents.

## arXiv: Gradient-Aware AI Agents for Autonomous Turbulence Model Discovery via Differentiable CFD
- In this work, we introduce a gradient-aware agentic framework that poses closure discovery as a bilevel problem: a multi-agent large language model (LLM) system searches over closure forms, while a GPU-native differentiable computational fluid dynamics (CFD) solver supplies gradients, making differentiable a posteriori learning an action of the autonomous AI agent.
- Turbulence modeling relies on human choices of closure form, physical inputs, and tunable parameters.
- Training closures a posteriori is efficient only with gradients through long chaotic rollouts, which few solvers provide.
- Most a posteriori approaches therefore resort to gradient-free optimization, and nearly all fix the closure form in advance, learning only its parameters.

## arXiv: Read and Ignore: What Self-Directed LLM Agents Do in Public Service Scenarios
- Underspecified instructions leave LLM agents to decide what to do.
- In public services, staff write these instructions, but the agent's decisions affect residents, patients, and students.
- We build four expert-validated public-service scenarios with records and decision levers that can be checked in the files, collect $2{,}400$ trajectories from ten LLMs under four prompt conditions, and compare them with $1{,}000$ runs in a context-free sandbox.
- Agents read the records in $96.9\%$ of open-ended runs and produce an artifact or a self-chosen task in $18.7\%$; with an explicit task, $86.0\%$ produce.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.

## arXiv: Where Autonomy Belongs: A Separation of Powers for Agentic Program Evolution
- LLM-driven program evolution rests on three decisions: govern directs the search from accumulated evidence, propose constructs the next candidate, and judge settles validity and fitness.
- Controller-centric systems strengthen govern and neglect the autonomy of propose, which caps what evolution can reach.
- Agent-centric systems let a coding agent take over both govern and propose, at a cognitive and computational cost.
- All three appear in every system, and existing work splits into two lines over who makes each.
- Neither line protects the independent authority of judge, so benchmark reliability suffers.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation methodology contribution — likely to influence future comparisons.

## arXiv: GuardianAgentFlow: Multi-Agent Coordination for Web Privacy Protection
