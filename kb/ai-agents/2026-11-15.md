## arXiv: GHA-SmellFix: Taking Action with Agentic LLMs for Detecting and Refactoring GitHub Actions Workflow Smells
- We present GHA-SmellFix, an agentic LLM pipeline for detecting and refactoring GitHub Actions workflow smells.
- Results show that decomposition does not universally improve patch quality; its main benefit is controlling when patch generation is attempted.
- Continuous integration and continuous delivery workflows are key artifacts in software projects.
- In GitHub Actions, they encode build, test, release, deployment, and security automation in YAML.
- These workflows contain configuration smells affecting security, reliability, maintainability, or operational stability.

**Why it matters:** Orchestration / coordination of many agents.

## arXiv: Open Challenges and Opportunities in Human-Agent Systems
- This paper examines how foundations developed by the Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (AAMAS) community inform these demands.
- Agentic AI systems increasingly undertake extended tasks and act on people's behalf, making human-agent interaction central to their effectiveness.
- Successful interaction requires understanding human perspectives, coordinating complementary capabilities, and maintaining productive relationships within socio-technical environments.
- We consider interactions ranging from individual human-agent partnerships to teams and larger groups, where participants differ in their roles, decision authority, capabilities, and objectives.
- We organize computational approaches around the connected challenges of understanding others and deciding how to interact with them.

## arXiv: RE-PRESENT: Evidence Triangulation across Alternative Candidate-Space Views for Efficient LLM Ensemble Inference
- Motivated by these findings, we introduce RE-PRESENT, a training-free framework that selectively re-presents disputed questions and directly aggregates candidate-level evidence across alternative views.
- Heterogeneous large language models (LLMs) exhibit complementary strengths, motivating routing and multi-model inference methods that seek to identify reliable predictions for each input.
- However, exploiting this complementarity remains challenging even when a correct prediction is available within the ensemble.
- We investigate this challenge across heterogeneous LLM ensembles and find that a simple single-round aggregation rule is highly competitive with learned routing methods, yet substantial oracle headroom remains.
- Further analysis shows that the evaluated single-round response signals provide limited discriminative power for identifying reliable predictions.

**Why it matters:** Evaluation methodology contribution — likely to influence future comparisons.
