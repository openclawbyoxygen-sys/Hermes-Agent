## arXiv: Learning Self-Orchestration in Decentralized Agent Swarms
- We introduce market-based post-training (MPT), a framework for jointly learning action policies and self-orchestration in decentralized agent swarms.
- Results show gains over monolithic post-training and confirm that agent participation indeed evolves with learning.
- The capabilities of an agent swarm depend on both its individual members and how they coordinate.
- Yet existing multi-agent systems often train agents within fixed orchestration structures, separating the learning of individual capabilities from the organization of collective behavior.
- Agents express their willingness to participate through bids, which determine who acts and how collective outcomes provide local training credit.

## arXiv: Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems
- In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs.
- Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration.
- However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift.
- The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path.

**Why it matters:** Tool-use pattern — relevant to agent-tool integration research.

## arXiv: Learning Rational Strategies for Temporal Objectives in Multi-Agent Systems
- We study rational synthesis for stochastic multi-agent systems with unknown dynamics, where each agent is equipped with its own probabilistic temporal objective specified using Probabilistic Linear Temporal Logic on Finite Traces ($\mathsf{PLTL}_f}$).
- We impose solution concepts (e.g., Nash equilibrium, subgame-perfect equilibrium) as constraints on the joint strategy profile, using payoffs derived from each agent's probability of satisfying its temporal objective.
- In particular, we formulate per-agent rational synthesis as finding a strategy profile -- if one exists -- such that (i) the designated agent's probabilistic temporal constraint is satisfied, and (ii) the profile represents a stable solution under a chosen solution concept.

**Why it matters:** Reinforcement-learning contribution within agent/decision-making.

## arXiv: Auditable and Deployable Multi-Agent Motion Planning via Posetal Games from Legal Guidelines
- To coordinate agents without explicitly communicating private preferences, we introduce belief play, which provably recovers the true equilibria from conservative local games, and a distributed iterative best-response protocol requiring less information.
- We present a framework for multi-agent motion planning under legal requirements based on games with partially ordered preferences (posetal games).
- When robots are deployed in the real-world, they must interact with other agents and accomplish their own goals while adhering to legal constraints.
- These potentially conflicting objectives are often only partially prioritized, making scalarized objectives or fully specified hierarchies unsuitable.

## arXiv: Decentralized LLM Workflows via Sequential Auctions
- We present a scalable framework for solving multi-step tasks through a decentralized market of self-interested agents.
- Across a diverse set of domains, we demonstrate that imperfect competition successfully sustains necessary agent profit margins while maintaining high task success rates at low costs.
- Prior theoretical work demonstrated that agents organized through sequential auctions can converge to a globally optimal policy in a Markov decision process (MDP), but relied on the restrictive assumption of the existence of perfect clones as a model of perfect competition.
- In reality, exact clones drive agent profit to zero, violating the natural risk-reward incentives of participants.

**Why it matters:** Reinforcement-learning contribution within agent/decision-making.
