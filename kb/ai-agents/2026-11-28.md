## arXiv: Soft Decentralized Expected SARSA: Equilibrium Selection via Gradual Adaptation
- We introduce Soft Decentralized Expected SARSA (SDES), a modification of Decentralized Q-learning in which agents maintain persistent exploration and update their acting Q-values gradually rather than switching directly to newly learned best responses when updating.
- We prove convergence to $ε$-greedy Nash equilibria in weakly acyclic stochastic games for sufficiently large batch sizes.
- We then show, in the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma with memory, that soft updating can reshape basins of attraction without changing the equilibrium set.
- In the deterministic limit, we derive analytical basin bounds governed by the Q-value differences between actions in the target policies and show that reducing the policy learning rate can favor either cooperative or competitive equilibria.

**Why it matters:** Reinforcement-learning contribution within agent/decision-making.

## arXiv: Do LLM Agents Compromise Like Humans? Risk and Responsibility in Dyadic Decision-Making
- Large language model (LLM)-based agents are increasingly deployed in settings where they must collaborate to make joint decisions.
- We investigate how this collaboration unfolds under risk, asking whether agents adjust their choices to accommodate their partners, how they assign responsibility for shared outcomes, and whether responsibility attribution shapes subsequent adjustment.
- We designed a series of agents with systematically varying risk preferences and paired them in a repeated joint decision-making task previously conducted with humans.
- Like humans, agents converged toward their partners' choices.
- However, similar aggregate behavior arose from different dynamics: agents showed little evidence of reciprocal adjustment, tended to weigh rewards more, and their responsibility attribution was decoupled from compromise.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to LLM-agent practitioners designing new systems.
