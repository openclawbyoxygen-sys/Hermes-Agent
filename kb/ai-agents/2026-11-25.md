## arXiv: ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control
- We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents.
- To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents.

## arXiv: From Local Errors to System Failures: Modeling and Benchmarking Error Propagation in Multi-Agent LLM Systems
- We propose Response-Aware Error Propagation among Agents (REPA), a structural model that connects measured local responses to system-level error propagation.
- In multi-agent systems (MAS) built on large language models (LLMs), errors can propagate through intermediate messages and compromise final answers.
- However, outcome-based evaluation obscures how agents amplify, attenuate, or correct these errors.
- REPA separately estimates input assembly and agent processing across multiple error channels, while theoretical analysis relates local modeling errors to endpoint prediction error.
- Experiments span three Qwen base models, six MAS workflows, and ten knowledge and reasoning settings in ErrPropBench-10.

## arXiv: From joint attention to shared symbols: A free-energy principle account
- How can attending to the same object help two people with different sensory experiences come to use a shared word?
- In a free-energy principle account, marginalizing the common object induces a statistical dependence between private perceptual states.
- In a categorical world, the joint prior inherits a mixture structure whose component index provides a candidate for shared reference.
- The collective predictive coding model approximates this prior by representing the dependence through that shared index.
- An established Metropolis-Hastings naming game lets agents infer and exchange words using private evidence.

## arXiv: Causal Evidence for the Emergence and Spread of Reward Hacking in AI Agent Swarms
- First, we show that cheating emerges spontaneously.
- Second, we show that agents who rarely cheat when working alone cheat most of the time after seeing a teammate's cheat, as 94% of teammates who saw an accepted cheat cheated.

## arXiv: AEP:Towards Trustworthy Agent Communication, Networking, and Collaboration
- We introduce Open Private Agent Collaboration (OPAC), an asset-centric paradigm for cooperation among private agents with open membership, partial observability, and ownership-aware access control.
- As privately owned agents become increasingly capable, collaboration in open agent networks is limited by partial topology knowledge, ownership boundaries, and the risk of exposing or contaminating private context.
- Existing agent-to-agent protocols are useful when collaborators are already known, while shared-memory and broadcast-based systems rely on visibility and trust assumptions that do not hold in decentralized, low-trust environments.
- Rather than treating collaboration as the exchange of final responses, OPAC enables agents to selectively discover, exchange, audit, and integrate policy-controlled context assets.

## arXiv: Social Interaction Breaks Replicate Independence in Controlled-Clone LLM Agents
- We introduce SocCogEval, a controlled-clone assay for Social Cognitive Transfer (SCT).
- Multi-agent LLM evaluations and social simulations create many agents from one model and treat them as independent replicates, even after the agents interact.
- This assumption remains untested with matched controls.
- Clones share the base model, system prompt, and decoding parameters and differ only in a private brief, and a factorial protocol separates the brief, 30 rounds of interaction, and initialization noise.
- On Qwen3-122B-FP8, interaction with the briefs held fixed raises off-topic BFI-10 response-profile drift by $\barΔ=+0.917$ and $+0.912$ in two topically disjoint scenarios (exact $p=0.0008$ and $0.0039$).
